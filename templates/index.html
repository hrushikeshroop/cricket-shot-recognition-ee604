Score explanation

The six conditional class scores describe the model's choice among its supported labels. Multiplying by the scope score gives the support used for thresholding. A high score can still be wrong.

Neither the scope model nor the threshold guarantees detection of every unusual shot.

Accuracy and review workload

Raw accuracy includes every supported-shot test clip. Automatic accuracy is measured only on cases accepted by the policy; deferred cases are not credited as correct.

Method and limits

Frozen CNNs provide full-video context and focused frame detail. Train-only dimensionality reduction and regularised heads learn six shot families. A binary scope model learns from separate non-target stroke examples. Calibration and threshold selection use different source groups.

Exactly one support score strictly above the fixed threshold gives an automatic label. Zero or multiple passing classes gives human review. Threshold targets are empirical development targets, rather than guarantees for new videos.

The benchmark reuses sources from the earlier eight-class study. Source grouping reduces known leakage, but match identities and all replay footage have not been independently verified. Fixed crops, ambiguous labels and unfamiliar cameras remain limitations.
